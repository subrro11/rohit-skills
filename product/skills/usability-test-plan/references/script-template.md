# Session script template

Times are for a 30-minute session. Scale each block for longer sessions, and check the minutes add up. Lines in quotes are read word for word; everything else is for the moderator.

```markdown
## Session script (<length> minutes)

### Before the session (moderator and note-taker)
- Reset the prototype: <how>. Load: <fixed date, scripted data>.
- Open the note grid at a new row for P<n>.
- Have the task cards ready to share: <list>.
- Check the participant can see and tap the prototype on <device and width>.

### 1. Intro (<n> min)
"Hi, I'm <first name>, and <first name> is here taking notes. Thanks for making the time.
We're trying out an early version of an app, and we want to see where it works and where it doesn't. We're testing the app, not you. If something's confusing, that's the app's fault, and it's exactly what we need to find.
There are no right or wrong answers, and you won't hurt anyone's feelings. This takes about <n> minutes."

### 2. Consent (<n> min)
"Before we start: with your permission, we'll record the screen and our voices. Only the team working on this will see the recording. We'll keep it for <period> and then delete it. Your name won't appear in any notes; we'll call you P<n>.
You can skip any question or stop at any time, and you'll still get the <incentive>.
Is it OK to record?"
- Start recording only after a clear yes. Say the consent back on the recording: "You've said it's OK to record."
- If they say no: run the session without recording and take fuller notes.

### 3. Warm-up (<n> min)
Two to four questions about recent real behaviour, never about the product.
- "Tell me about the last time <situation>."
- "What did you do about it?"
- "What happened next?"

### 4. Tasks (<n> min)
"I'll give you a few things to try. Please do them as you normally would. For some I'll ask you to talk me through what you're thinking as you go; for others, just do it and we'll talk after."

For each task:
- **Task <n> (<minutes>, <think aloud / silent then talk>):** "<scenario, word for word>"
- Starting state: <screen, data>
- Card to share: <if any>
- Ends when: <observable end>
- If stuck: wait <n> seconds of silence, then "What are you looking for?" If still stuck after <n> more seconds: "What would you do next if I weren't here?" After <limit>, move on and mark fail.
- Probes (after the task): <two or three, from the task-writing guide>

### 5. Comprehension check (<n> min)
- "<question that checks they understood what the product did>"

### 6. Debrief (<n> min)
- "What, if anything, would you change?"
- "Thinking about your real week, when would you open this, if at all?"
- "Is there anything else we should know?"
"Thanks so much. <How and when the incentive arrives.> If you have questions about the recording later, <contact route>."

### After the session (moderator and note-taker, 10 min)
- Fill the grid gaps while it's fresh. Agree the outcome level and any severity.
- Note anything the moderator said that may have led the participant.
- Reset the prototype.
```

## Neutral prompts the moderator may use

- "What are you thinking?"
- "What are you looking for?"
- "What would you do next?"
- "Say more about that."
- "What did you expect to happen?"
- Repeating their last words back as a question.

## Things the moderator must not say

- Any button, tab or screen name before the participant uses it.
- "Good", "right" or "perfect" after an action: it tells them they're on track.
- "Most people…", "It's easy, just…", "Did you notice…?"
- Answers to "Is this right?" Reply: "What do you think?"
