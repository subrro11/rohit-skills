# Evidence ledger

Every finding gets one row. The report cites rows by ID, so a reader can check any sentence against its source.

## Format

| ID | Stage | Claim | Source | Quote | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Company, Problem, Competitors, News or Trends | The claim in your words, with its number, year, sample and denominator | Publisher, title, date published or updated, link, date read | A short verbatim quote (under 30 words) that backs up the claim | High, Medium or Low, plus a reason |

- **One claim per row.** Split "X is common and growing" into two rows.
- **The quote must contain the claim.** If you can't quote it, you haven't verified it.
- **News rows carry the event date** as well as the publication date, if they differ.
- **Keep the source's own terms.** If it says "rotated shifts", don't write "worked nights".

## Confidence

Grade each row, then grade each answer in the report from the rows under it.

- **High**: a primary source states the claim directly, the quote says exactly that, and the data is recent enough for the decision. A source's own statement about itself (a company describing its product, a review describing its own limits) is High for *what it says*, not for whether it's true.
- **Medium**: one good source with a known weakness (secondary, old, small sample, undated, or the primary wouldn't load), or two sources that broadly agree but measure slightly different things.
- **Low**: a single practitioner or self-reported source, a figure the source itself hedges, traction read from a handful of ratings, or anything inferred from what a page *doesn't* say.
- **Agreement raises a grade by one step** only when the sources are independent. A reprint, a syndicated copy or two articles quoting the same release count as one source.
- **Age lowers a grade by one step** when the market moves fast and the data is more than two years old.
- **Split a row when it has two grades.** A competitor's listing can be High for what it claims to do and Low for traction. Grade each part separately.

A section's grade is the grade of the weakest row its answer depends on. Rows that only add colour don't count, and a section is never graded higher than its evidence.

## Found, not read

A second table for leads you couldn't open or didn't have time to read: source, what it was expected to show, and why it wasn't read (paywall, bot check, 403, out of time). Never cite these in the report. Each one should either become an "Evidence needed:" line or be dropped.
